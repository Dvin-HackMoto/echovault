– PRODUCT –

**TITLE:** ECHOVAULT 
**INSPIRATION:**

PROBLEM:
Individuals experiencing memory loss may struggle with remembering:

* Names and relationships of family members
* Daily routines and appointments
* Important conversations and events
* Medication schedules
* Personal information and familiar places
* Activities they have already completed

Because of these challenges, they may become more dependent on caregivers.
Existing AI assistants can provide information, pero hindi sila necessarily designed around verified personal memories, cognitive accessibility, and offline functionality.

### Problems we want to address

**Problem 1: Memory Difficulties**
Users may struggle to recall important personal information during daily activities.
**Problem 2: Limited Independence**
Some users need assistance from caregivers even for simple questions about their schedules or family members.
**Problem 3: Privacy Concerns**
Personal information such as family relationships, medical routines, and private conversations is sensitive.
**Problem 4: Internet Dependency**
Cloud-based AI systems may become unavailable when internet connectivity is poor or nonexistent.
**Problem 5: Caregiver Workload**
Caregivers may need to repeatedly explain schedules, relationships, and routines.

OBJECTIVE:

### General Objective

To develop an offline AI-powered mobile memory assistant that supports individuals experiencing cognitive difficulties through personalized memory retrieval, accessible interaction, and caregiver-managed information.

### Specific Objectives

1. Develop a mobile application capable of running an AI language model locally.
2. Implement an on-device memory database for storing and retrieving personal information.
3. Create an AI chatbot that answers questions using locally stored memories.
4. Develop a caregiver interface for managing personal records and daily schedules.
5. Implement offline synchronization between nearby patient and caregiver devices.
6. Provide voice-based interaction for users who have difficulty typing.
7. Develop an accessible interface with simplified navigation.
8. Implement local encryption and appropriate caregiver access controls.
9. Evaluate the accuracy, usability, privacy, and performance of the application.
10. Categorize memories by type, importance, trust status, and validity so retrieval and presentation can prioritize relevant, reliable information.
11. Allow caregivers to select topics for memory games and trivia based on the user's interests and observed everyday memory-support needs, without presenting these activities as clinical assessments.



TARGET USERS:

### Primary Users: Patients

Individuals experiencing:

* Early-stage Alzheimer's disease
* Mild Cognitive Impairment
* Memory-related difficulties following TBI
* Other memory difficulties where a personalized memory aid may be useful

### Secondary Users: Caregivers

* Family members
* Personal caregivers
* Authorized guardians
* Professional caregivers

The application should support users with different levels of independence.

**FEATURES:**

### A. Patient Application — EchoVault

**1. Offline AI Memory Assistant**
Allows users to ask questions about their personal information, family relationships, daily activities, and important events. The AI retrieves answers from locally stored, verified memories without requiring an internet connection.
**2. Personal Memory Database**
Stores and organizes important personal information, including:

* Family members, friends, and relationships
* Birthdays, anniversaries, and significant events
* Daily routines and personal preferences
* Appointments and scheduled activities
* Familiar places and important personal details

Each memory is organized using four separate dimensions:

* **Category:** Identity and relationships; daily routines and schedules; personal history and experiences; preferences and familiar places; care and safety information; or learning and engagement.
* **Importance:** Critical, important, or general. Importance is separate from urgency.
* **Trust status:** Verified, unverified, conflicting, or outdated.
* **Validity:** Persistent, scheduled, temporary, or archived.

Records should also retain relevant metadata such as source, creation/update timestamps, and applicable validity dates. Schedules and medication reminders should use dedicated structured records rather than relying only on free-text memories.
**3. Daily Schedule and Reminders**
Displays the user's daily activities and provides scheduled reminders for appointments, meals, family visits, and medication routines. Reminders operate locally on the device, even without internet access.
**4. Voice-Based Interaction**
Allows users to ask questions and hear responses through voice input and audio output, reducing the need for typing. Voice functionality may support Filipino and English, subject to offline speech-recognition capabilities.
**5. Family Photo Directory**
Displays photographs of family members and familiar people alongside their names, relationships, and relevant personal information. This helps users review who people are without relying entirely on memory recall.
**6. Daily Memory Games**
Provides short, optional activities designed to encourage users to recall and recognize information in a simple, engaging way.
Examples include:

* **Family Matching:** Match family members' names with their photographs.
* **Name Recall:** Identify a familiar person from their photograph or saved profile.
* **Event Recall:** Answer questions about previously recorded events or visits.
* **Routine Recall:** Identify the next activity in a familiar daily routine.
* **Picture Matching:** Match related objects, photographs, or familiar items.
* **Personal Memory Quiz:** Answer simple questions based on verified information stored in the user's memory database.

Caregivers can choose the topics or memory categories covered by games—for example, family names, relationships, familiar places, routines, or recent events. If a caregiver notices that the user often has difficulty recalling a particular topic in everyday life, they can prioritize gentle activities related to that topic. This is caregiver-directed personalization, not automated diagnosis or proof of cognitive decline.

Games should have adjustable difficulty, clear instructions, encouraging feedback, and an option to skip or stop at any time. Results can inform activity preferences but must not be presented as a medical diagnosis or cognitive assessment.
**7. Pop-Up Trivia and Memory Prompts**
Displays occasional, brief, optional questions or familiar facts throughout the day to encourage engagement and provide gentle opportunities to revisit information.
Examples include:

* **Personal Trivia:** "Do you remember who visited you last Sunday?"
* **Family Trivia:** "Ana is your daughter. Do you remember her favorite flower?"
* **Daily Routine Prompt:** "It's almost lunchtime. What do you usually do before eating?"
* **Past Event Recall:** "You celebrated your birthday with your family last month. Would you like to look at the photos?"
* **General Trivia:** Simple questions about familiar objects, everyday life, nature, or common knowledge.

Caregivers can select the topics, memory categories, and frequency of prompts. For example, if a caregiver observes that the user often forgets family relationships or daily routines, they can choose more gentle prompts about those topics. This setting reflects caregiver observations and preferences; it must not be described as a clinical assessment. Personalized prompts must be based on verified information. General trivia should use locally stored questions and answers so that it works offline. Prompts should appear at configurable intervals and avoid interrupting sleep, appointments, or other important activities.
**8. Simplified and Accessible Interface**
Provides an interface designed for ease of use, featuring:

* Large buttons and readable text
* Clear icons and high-contrast elements
* Minimal navigation and simple screens
* Voice interaction and read-aloud responses
* Easy access to memories, schedules, and games
* Adjustable reminder frequency and activity difficulty

**9. Personal Orientation Screen**
Presents helpful information such as the current date, time, scheduled activities, familiar people, and the user's daily routine. This provides a simple reference point when the user needs help orienting themselves.

### B. Caregiver Application — EchoVault Care

**10. Caregiver Dashboard**
Provides authorized caregivers with a centralized interface for managing the user's profile, personal memories, family directory, daily schedule, reminders, and activity settings.
**11. Caregiver Memory Management**
Allows caregivers to add, edit, verify, and remove personal information. Caregivers can also review AI-suggested memories before they are added to the verified memory database.
**12. Medication Reminder and Confirmation Tracking**
Allows caregivers to configure medication schedules and track whether a dose was confirmed as taken, remains unconfirmed, or was confirmed as skipped. The application does not prescribe medication or determine whether a dose was actually taken solely because a reminder appeared.
**13. Memory Game and Trivia Customization**
Allows caregivers to select suitable memory games, adjust difficulty, manage the frequency of pop-up questions, and add personalized questions based on verified family information and significant events. Caregivers can also choose specific topics or memory categories to practice based on the user's interests and their observations of everyday recall difficulties. This supports tailored engagement but does not diagnose or measure a medical condition.
**14. Activity and Engagement History**
Records basic information about completed games, answered prompts, skipped activities, and user preferences. Caregivers can review this history to understand which activities the user engages with most often and adjust selected topics accordingly. Activity history must not be treated as a validated measure of cognitive ability or used to diagnose a condition.
**15. Caregiver-Managed Mode**
Allows caregivers to configure a simplified experience for users who need additional assistance. The application can prioritize daily schedules, familiar photographs, essential reminders, and selected memory activities.
**16. Offline Device Synchronization**
Allows authorized caregivers to transfer updated memories, schedules, family photographs, and activity settings to the patient's device through a nearby connection, such as Bluetooth or Wi-Fi Direct, without relying on cloud services.

### C. Privacy, Security, and Reliability

**17. Local AI Processing**
Runs the AI model and core memory retrieval functionality directly on the device, reducing dependence on external AI services and internet connectivity.
**18. Encrypted Local Storage**
Protects personal information using local database encryption and device-level security mechanisms.
**19. Caregiver Access Controls**
Restricts access to sensitive information and memory-management functions to authorized caregivers.
**20. Categorized and Verified Memory Retrieval**
Organizes memories by category, importance, trust status, and validity. Retrieval considers the user's question, relevance, verification, and time sensitivity. If information is missing, conflicting, outdated, or unverified, the assistant should acknowledge the limitation rather than silently choosing or inventing an answer.
**21. Offline Operation**
Keeps core functions available without an internet connection, including personal memory retrieval, locally scheduled reminders, stored family photographs, and preloaded memory games and trivia.
**22. Local Backup and Recovery**
Provides an option to export and restore personal records through a protected local backup process, helping reduce the risk of losing important information if a device is replaced or reset.



**SCOPE AND LIMITATIONS:**

### Scope

EchoVault is an Android-first, offline AI-powered personal memory assistant designed to support individuals experiencing memory difficulties and help caregivers manage essential personal information. It prioritizes privacy, accessibility, and reliable access to verified memories without requiring an internet connection.
The prototype will include:

* **Offline AI Memory Assistant:** Answers questions using locally stored personal memories and information.
* **Categorized Personal Memory Database:** Stores and organizes information by category, importance, trust status, and validity, including source and relevant timestamps. Schedules and medication reminders use structured records.
* **Caregiver Dashboard:** Allows caregivers to add, edit, verify, and remove personal memories and manage the user's settings.
* **Daily Schedule and Reminders:** Provides reminders for appointments, daily activities, and medication routines.
* **Caregiver-Directed Memory Games:** Offers optional activities such as name recall, family matching, picture matching, and personal memory quizzes. Caregivers can select the topics or categories to focus on based on the user's interests and observed everyday recall difficulties.
* **Caregiver-Configured Trivia and Memory Prompts:** Presents occasional personal memory questions, family trivia, and preloaded general trivia. Caregivers can select topic categories and adjust frequency based on the user's preferences and support needs.
* **Family Photo Directory:** Displays familiar people alongside their names and relationships.
* **Accessible User Interface:** Provides simplified navigation, readable text, clear controls, and optional voice interaction, depending on implementation feasibility.
* **Local Data Protection:** Stores personal information on the device with appropriate security measures.
* **Nearby Offline Synchronization:** Supports transferring selected information between paired devices through Bluetooth or Wi-Fi Direct, subject to device compatibility.
* **Local Backup and Recovery:** Supports protected local backup and restoration where feasible.

### Limitations

* **No Internet-Dependent Services:** EchoVault is designed for offline use. Remote access, cloud synchronization, and real-time updates across distant devices are outside the prototype's scope.
* **Limited to Recorded Information:** The assistant cannot reliably answer questions about personal experiences or details that have not been recorded in its local memory database.
* **Potential AI Errors:** The local AI may misunderstand questions or generate inaccurate responses. The system should prioritize relevant, verified memories and acknowledge when information is unavailable or uncertain.
* **Memory Classification Requires Careful Review:** Incorrect categories, importance levels, or validity dates can affect retrieval and reminders. Caregivers should be able to review and correct records, and safety-critical information must come from authorized sources.
* **Caregiver Topic Selection Is Not Diagnosis:** A caregiver may prioritize a topic because they observe everyday recall difficulties, but game and trivia performance must not be used to infer or diagnose cognitive decline.
* **Device Hardware Constraints:** Performance depends on the phone's processing power, memory, storage capacity, and compatibility with the selected AI model.
* **Voice Recognition Limitations:** Offline speech recognition may struggle with background noise, accents, or mixed-language speech, depending on the model used.
* **Limited Synchronization Range:** Nearby synchronization requires compatible devices to be physically close enough to establish a connection. Updates will not automatically reach caregivers who are elsewhere.
* **No Medical Diagnosis or Treatment:** EchoVault is not intended to diagnose, treat, or monitor the progression of Alzheimer's disease, mild cognitive impairment, or other medical conditions.
* **Memory Games Are Not Clinical Assessments:** Games and trivia are intended for optional engagement and personal memory practice. Their results must not be interpreted as medical evaluations or evidence of cognitive improvement.
* **Reminders Do Not Confirm Real-World Actions:** A medication reminder or confirmation button cannot independently verify that a medication was taken or that an activity was completed.
* **Caregiver Involvement Remains Necessary:** The system is intended to complement, not replace, human caregiving, professional medical advice, or emergency services.

## CORE USER JOURNEY

### 1\. Caregiver Setup and Profile Creation

The caregiver creates the user's local profile, configures accessibility preferences, and enters essential information such as family members, familiar places, routines, appointments, and important personal memories.

### 2\. Categorize, Prioritize, and Verify Memories

As information is added, it is organized by **category** (what it is), **importance** (how consequential it is), **trust status** (how reliable it is), and **validity** (when it applies). The caregiver verifies records and checks time-sensitive details. Conflicting or outdated information is flagged for review rather than silently treated as current.

### 3\. Configure Personalized Games and Trivia

The caregiver selects topics for memory games and trivia—for example, family names, relationships, familiar places, routines, or meaningful events. If they notice the user often has difficulty recalling a particular topic in everyday situations, they can choose to include more gentle activities about that topic. This is a personalization setting based on caregiver observations, not a diagnosis or clinical assessment.

### 4\. Daily Home Screen

The user opens EchoVault and sees a simple home screen with the date, relevant upcoming activities, familiar people, and easy access to memory assistance, reminders, and optional activities. Time-sensitive information is surfaced when relevant rather than treating every stored memory equally.

### 5\. Offline Memory Assistance

When the user asks a question, EchoVault identifies the likely topic and retrieves relevant local records. It checks verification status and validity before responding. For example, it uses structured schedule records to answer “What is my next appointment?” and verified relationship records to answer “Who is Ana?” If information is missing, conflicting, or uncertain, it says so rather than inventing an answer.

### 6\. Reminders and Daily Activities

EchoVault provides locally scheduled reminders for appointments, routines, and medication schedules. The user can acknowledge a reminder, and the application records that response without treating it as proof that the activity or medication was completed.

### 7\. Daily Memory Games

At a suitable time, the user can choose or receive an optional short activity based on caregiver-selected topics, such as matching family photos to names or recalling a familiar routine. Activities use verified information, provide encouraging feedback, and can be skipped or stopped at any time.

### 8\. Occasional Trivia and Memory Prompts

EchoVault presents brief, optional prompts at caregiver-configured intervals. Prompts may focus on selected personal topics or use preloaded general trivia. They avoid sleep, appointments, and other configured quiet periods, and should not make the user feel tested or pressured.

### 9\. Caregiver Review and Updates

The caregiver reviews and updates memories, corrects outdated details, resolves conflicting records, adjusts schedules, and changes game or trivia topics as the user's interests and support needs change. Activity history can help the caregiver understand preferences, but it is not treated as a clinical measure.

### 10\. Offline Synchronization and Ongoing Support

When the user's and caregiver's devices are nearby, supported changes can be synchronized through a compatible local connection. Until then, updates remain local. The user continues using EchoVault for everyday memory support, while caregivers remain available when the application cannot answer reliably or additional help is needed.
